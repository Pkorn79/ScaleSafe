# Page patterns

## Quick business site

Use when a service business needs a credible web presence quickly.

Recommended structure:

1. Business name or literal offer, primary action, and concrete supporting copy.
2. Who it helps and what outcome it supports.
3. Services or offers with clear differences.
4. Process or next steps.
5. Evidence supplied by the business.
6. FAQ that resolves genuine buying friction.
7. Final action and contact details.

## Lead-generation landing page

Keep one conversion. Remove competing navigation when appropriate. Explain the offer, qualification, what happens next, and privacy expectations near the form. Connect the form to a real destination before presenting it as working.

## Download or resource hub

Use when visitors must choose and install files, templates, skills, or workshop materials.

- Lead with the resources, not a general marketing pitch.
- Group downloads by product, platform, or use case.
- Name file type and compatibility.
- Give no more than three initial setup steps.
- State prerequisites before the visitor downloads.
- Provide direct links and optional checksums.
- Include one starter prompt or first action when the resource needs activation.

## Product or SaaS site

Show the actual product state early. Explain the workflow in the order the customer experiences it. Use sanitized real screenshots rather than generic mockups. Keep claims bounded and distinguish assistance from guarantees.

## Event or workshop page

Lead with the event name, date, audience, and registration action. Include agenda, outcomes, presenter context, logistics, replay or resource terms, and the post-event action.

## Portfolio

Lead with the person or studio and the work itself. Use inspectable project media, a concise role statement, project context, and a direct contact path. Avoid generic biography before the work.

## Documentation and support

Open to search, navigation, common tasks, and escalation paths. Favor concise procedural pages, clear prerequisites, and warnings near irreversible actions. Separate product guidance from legal or security disclosures.

## Tool or application

Build the usable tool as the first screen. Prioritize predictable navigation, stable controls, data states, and repeated task efficiency. Do not replace the application with a promotional landing page.
